5 Ways to Style an Oversized Tee (Without Looking Like a Potato)

Aanya Kapoor·Feb 14, 2026·4 min read

The oversized tee is the most versatile piece in your wardrobe — if you know how to work it. We break down the top five moves.

The oversized tee is simultaneously the easiest and most misunderstood piece in menswear. Wear it right, and you look effortlessly pulled-together. Wear it wrong, and you just look like you grabbed your older brother's shirt by accident. The difference is almost always in the proportions.

1. Tuck + Taper

A half-tuck or full front-tuck with slim or tapered trousers immediately adds intention. Your brain reads “I meant to do that” instead of “I rolled out of bed.” Keep the tuck loose — one finger inside the waistband, pull slightly, done. Don't iron it.

“Volume in one place, structure in another. That's the whole formula.”

2. Layer It Open

An open button-up or linen shirt worn over or under the tee turns one item into a complete look. Open-shirt layering adds length, breaks colour, and gives you something to take off when the temperature rises. A lightweight flannel in autumn does the same thing.

3. Cropped Bottoms

Pair your oversized tee with a cropped, wide-leg, or tapered trouser. The exposed ankle creates a visual break that stops the look from feeling like one big blob. Cargo pants with a drawstring waist are a perfect counterpart — they're utilitarian against the softness of a tee, and the pockets add visual interest where the tee has none.

4. The Monochrome Move

Wearing a tee and bottom in the same colour family — sand on sand, black on black, grey on grey — immediately elevates the read. Tonal dressing makes the silhouette itself the statement, not the colours fighting each other.

5. Break the Waist with an Accessory

A simple canvas belt, a crossbody bag sitting at the hip, or a watch that peeks out from a rolled sleeve — all of these break the visual monotony of a big tee and establish a waist where the tee has none. Small detail, major difference.
